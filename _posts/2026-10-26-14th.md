The 14th is a neighborhood of crisp mornings and hot afternoons. Sometimes, the wind rushes up from the Seine so hard that the ground is scattered with leaves from the trees that line the few wide boulevards. But mostly the air is tender and still, giving mornings a wakeful cool. It's the perfect feeling for a stroll. 

The lion of Belfort's cage is under construction. [^lion] For several years, workers have been revamping the streets, bike lanes, trees, and sidewalks around Place Denfert Rochereau. The big cat is therefore inaccessible, but he still stands over the center of the 14th to keep watch. 

It's not entirely clear whether his pose is one of rest--lying down--or of watchfulness, or even whether he is in the middle of standing up, perhaps to defend his roost. This ambiguity and the hint of motion that goes along with it make the statue all the more powerful. It's a good reflection of the neighborhood, which can never quite decide if it's more in motion or still. The commercial corridors and metro lines contrast quite strongly with the very quiet, tree- and cat-lined streets. 

There's a building that always catches my eye at 23 rue Bezout, an anonymous small street with little else remarkable about it. It's slightly smaller than the typical Parisian apartment complex, and it's lined principally with white tiles; mixed in are some patterns of baby blue, yellow, and red ones too. [^bezout] I've never seen another building like it in Paris. It looks like a baby of the modernist period, but it seems to have been built in 1987. It's not famous, and I can find no one discussing it. Just a block away, a front door to an apartment complex is more like a porthole, with a rounded top and circular window. It's baby blue and seems like it will lead to another dimension. [^door]

These are some of my favorite Parisian streets. Nobody knows about them, so they are quiet, with unheralded but highly impressive unique structures and adornments.
